\reviewer
\begin{generalcomment}
	This paper proposes a constrained optimization-based neuro-adaptive control (CONAC) method for Euler-Lagrange systems under weight and input constraints, with real-time experimental validation. The integration of DNN approximation within a constrained optimization framework is interesting. However, several critical issues must be addressed before the paper can be considered for publication.
\end{generalcomment}

\begin{revcomment}[1][1]{}
	The main contribution lacks clarity. The paper claims to handle both weight and input constraints simultaneously, but it fails to explain why this is necessary or how it improves upon existing constrained adaptive control methods (e.g., barrier Lyapunov functions, projection algorithms). The novelty over the authors’ prior work \cite{Ryu:2025aa}, \cite{Ryu:2025ab} is not sufficiently justified.
\end{revcomment}

\begin{revresponse}
  We concur with the reviewer that the main contribution of the paper was not clearly articulated in the previous submission. 
  In the previous version, the major contribution was stated as handling both weight and convex input constraints simultaneously in a unified constrained optimization framework.
  However, we acknowledge that, over the previously stated contribution, the significance of the proposed method is to incorporate multiple convex input constraints into the NAC framework, which typically considers only the element-wise input constraints, as compared in Fig.~1 of the revised manuscript, as shown below:
  \begin{center}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.65\linewidth]{figs/r1c1_1.png}
  \end{center}

  Moreover, to clarify the novelty of the proposed method, we have categorized the existing input-constraint handling approaches into two methods: Structural Methods and Optimization-Based Methods in Introduction section, as shown in Table~I.
  \begin{center}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.99\linewidth]{figs/r1c1_2.png}
    % \smallskip
    % {\small\itshape Excerpt from the revised manuscript: Table I.}
  \end{center}
  In the table, the pros and cons of each method are discussed, and the novelty of the proposed method is clearly stated as follows:
  \begin{itemize}

    \item 
      Rigorous stability guarantee using Lyapunov-friendly structure like structural methods.

    \item 
      Capability to handle multiple convex input constraints simultaneously, which is not possible with existing structural methods.

    \item 
      Continuous adaptation without requiring numerical solver, resulting in typically low computational burden.

  \end{itemize}
  
  \bigskip

  Particularly, since our main focus is on the input saturation problem, barrier lyapunov function (BLF)-based methods are not considered in the comparison.
  In the literature, BLF-based methods are typically used to handle state constraints, and additionally incorporate the structural methods such as auxiliary system or smooth approximation methods to handle input constraints, as well.
  Moreover, projection methods were not included in the comparison because the input-feasible set induced in the full DNN-weight space is generally highly nonlinear owing to the nonlinear dependence of the DNN output on its hidden-layer weights.
  Consequently, the projection methods are not typically employed in the NAC literature, and the comparison with the projection methods is not straightforward.

  \bigskip

  The justification of the novelty over our prior works \cite{Ryu:2025aa}, \cite{Ryu:2025ab} is also clarified in lines 200-210 of the revised manuscript, as follows:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item 
      The input constraints considered in the previous work \cite{Ryu:2025aa} were limited to norm constraints, which are a specific type of convex constraint.
      In contrast, the revised manuscript generalized the input constraints to multiple convex constraints, which can represent a wider range of practical saturations on the control input.

    \item 
      Previously, the validations were conducted only on numerical simulations.
      In the revised manuscript, we have conducted real-time experimental validations on a 2-link robotic manipulator system, which demonstrates the applicability in real-world scenarios.

    \item 
      Due to the high mathematical complexity of the DNN, the previous works \cite{Ryu:2025aa}, \cite{Ryu:2025ab} employed single hidden layer NNs, while the revised manuscript employs DNN with multiple hidden layers.
      Moreover, the rigorous stability analysis has been extended to accommodate the DNN structure.
      
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{1.2em}

  We hope that these revisions provide a clearer and more focused presentation of the contributions and novelty of our work.

\end{revresponse}

\begin{revcomment}[1][1]{}
	What will the control signal represent at the extremes of these bounds? It will correspond to the Lyapunov function. How does it prevent breaches of constraints?
\end{revcomment}

\begin{revresponse}
  We appreciate this observation.
  As we mentioned in previous response (Comment 2), the proposed method handles the constraints in a soft manner using continuous adaptation of the DNN weights and Lagrange multipliers.
  Therefore, abrupt changes in the control signal are typically avoided, and the constraints will be eventually satisfied along the adaptation process.

  \bigskip

  However, the control signal potentially exhibits high-frequency oscillations at the extremes of the bounds, as discussed in Remark~4 of the revised manuscript, depending on the parameters of the adaptation laws, $\alpha$ and $\beta_i,\beta_j$.

  Therefore, the Implicit Function Theorem cannot be directly invoked to establish the existence of the ideal control input at that boundary.
  We acknowledge that this limitation was not clearly stated in the previous conference paper, and we appreciate the reviewer for bringing this issue to our attention.

  \bigskip

  We emphasize, however, that the conference paper was provided solely as related reference material and that the present manuscript does not rely on its Implicit-Function-Theorem-based existence argument. 
  In the present manuscript, the ideal control law is defined directly under the assumed nonsingularity of the control-effectiveness matrix. 
  Therefore, the identified limitation of the previous conference paper does not affect the theoretical results established in the present manuscript.

\end{revresponse}

\begin{revresponse}
  We thank the reviewer for suggesting these recent studies. 
  We have examined them from a constrained-optimization perspective and clarified their differences from the proposed CONAC framework.

  \bigskip

  First, Zhang \etal \cite{Zhang:2024ab} formulated adaptive dynamic programming (ADP) problems for unknown nonlinear systems with actuator failures and asymmetric input constraints.
  They designed the infinite-horizon cost function including CBF-based safety constraints, input saturation, and actuator-failure.
  Then, the Hamilton-Jacobi-Isaacs (HJI) equation is solved to derive the optimal control law using zero-sum game theory, \ie minimizing the cost function with respect to the control input and maximizing it with respect to the actuator-failure input.
  The optimal cost function is approximated by a critic neural network (CNN) and the optimal control law is derived from the CNN, according to the HJI equation.
  In particular, the asymmetric input constraints are handled by introducing a non-quadratic function in the cost function, which is designed to result in an asymmetric bounded function with $\tanh(\cdot)$, ensuring that the control law satisfies the prescribed box input constraints.

  Similarly, Qin \etal \cite{Qin:2025aa} formulated the ADP problem for interconnected nonlinear systems with uncertainties induced by the interconnections.
  In this paper, the cost function is formulated to be maximized with respect to the uncertainties, instead of the actuator-failure input in \cite{Zhang:2024ab}.
  The CNN is used to approximate the optimal cost function like \cite{Zhang:2024ab}, and the optimal control law is derived from the CNN.

  In addition, Qin \etal \cite{Qin:2025ab} also proposed similar control design which reshapes the constrained system into an unconstrained system by using a smooth mapping function.
  In terms of the input saturation, symmetric input constraints are considered similarly to \cite{Zhang:2024ab}.
  Unlike \cite{Zhang:2024ab} and \cite{Qin:2025aa}, the optimal control law is derived from the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation, instead of the HJI equation, and the optimal cost function is approximated by a CNN similarly to \cite{Zhang:2024ab} and \cite{Qin:2025aa}.

  \bigskip

  They all formulated the constrained optimization problem based on the ADP framework.
  The optimal control law is derived from HJI or HJB equation, and the optimal cost function is approximated by a CNN.
  Therefore, the control law can be derived from the CNN directly.
  However, they are all limited to the specific types of input constraints, \ie asymmetric or symmetric box constraints, and cannot generally handle other types of convex input constraints.
  In contrast, the proposed CONAC framework can handle a broader class of convex input constraints, including element-wise bounds, norm constraints, and other coupled convex input constraints, by explicitly incorporating the admissible input set into the constrained optimization problem and deriving the adaptation laws from the primal-dual formulation.

  In terms of the objective function, since the proposed method is based on instantaneous tracking-error minimization, it may be theoretically enhanced by considering the infinite-horizon cost function like \cite{Zhang:2024ab, Qin:2025aa, Qin:2025ab}.
  Moreover, utilizing game theory to handle actuator failures or uncertainties, as in \cite{Zhang:2024ab, Qin:2025aa}, may also be a potential extension of the proposed method.
